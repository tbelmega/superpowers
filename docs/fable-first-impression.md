The core disposition: subtraction as the primary design act. The centerpiece document isn't a feature list, it's MINIMIZATION.md — a per-item keep/drop ledger where every deletion gets an argued verdict. This is someone who gets more intellectual satisfaction from a justified removal than from building something new, and who treats "the model already does this" as a falsifiable claim to be checked feature-by-feature rather than a vibe. Fourteen skills became seven, and the burden of proof was on staying, not going.

A depreciation thesis, held with conviction. The whole fork rests on one bet: prompt scaffolding is a depreciating asset. As labs train capabilities into models and harnesses absorb workflows natively (plan mode, subagents, worktrees, code review), guidance that duplicates them goes from helpful to harmful — "guidance that contradicts training degrades performance" is stated as a design law, not a hunch. What survives the purge is telling: the seven keepers all target what I'd call model character flaws rather than capability gaps — coding before understanding, skipping the failing test, patching symptoms, claiming unverified success, sycophancy under review pressure. The author's implicit claim is that capability improves with each model generation but temperament doesn't, so temperament-correcting discipline is the only durable layer.

An economist's relationship to tokens. "Length is a cost" appears in nearly every document, elevated to a first-class constraint alongside correctness. The two-layer architecture (cheap always-on essences in AGENTS.md, expensive depth loaded on judgment) is basically a caching strategy for guidance. MEMORY.md even reveals the underlying material reality: three paid subscriptions (Claude, Codex, Cursor) and a desire to arbitrage usage limits across them. This isn't abstract minimalism — it's cost discipline from someone who pays the bill.

Trust in the model over enforcement of the model. The most philosophically interesting move is dropping the SessionStart hook and bootstrap — the enforcement machinery — and replacing it with plain text plus "loading is the model's judgment, not enforced." That's consistent with the grain principle but it's also the riskiest bet in the repo: the original hook existed precisely because skill auto-triggering is probabilistic. The author chose coherence with the thesis over a safety net, and noted that the hook "over-triggered on simple tasks" as the justification. Convention over mechanism, everywhere: multi-harness support by shared-file convention rather than packaging, distribution by symlink rather than marketplace.

Epistemic hygiene that most repos don't have. Decided vs. deferred vs. provisional are carefully distinguished ("KEEP (trim deferred)", a visible TODO comment in AGENTS.md, "this Status section is authoritative where it differs from the analysis below"). MEMORY.md is written as a handoff to a future session — the author treats agent sessions as ephemeral workers and durable docs as the real state, which means the project practices the discipline it prescribes. Nothing is claimed more finished than it is; README says outright "the current focus is the shape, not final wording."

Understatement as identity. The name — decently-capable-powers, previously distinctly-adequate-powers — is a deliberate deflation of "superpowers." Paired with the genuinely gracious attribution to Jesse Vincent (sponsor link included), it signals someone skeptical of hype who frames the fork not as "the original was wrong" but "the world moved and absorbed it." Respectful, but unsentimental about deleting 90% of what they forked.

The tension I'd flag: the thesis says heavy machinery is dead weight, yet the roadmap (UPDATE.md distribution agent, model-selection decision matrix, spec-carried guidance) is quietly accreting new machinery of its own. The author's discipline will be tested less by what they cut from obra's repo and more by what they decline to add to their own.
